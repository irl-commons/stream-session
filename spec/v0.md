# stream-session v0

Draft, November 2026. MIT licensed.

The key words MUST, MUST NOT, SHOULD, SHOULD NOT and MAY are used as in
RFC 2119 and RFC 8174.

Parts marked **Bench (two owners)** describe behaviour that can't be proven
with one person's gear. They stay draft until two owners' devices have run
them in one show (section 19).

## 1. What it's for

A live show often involves more than one device, and more and more often
more than one person. One streamer starts on the PC at home, walks out with
the phone and ends on a backpack box. Two streamers meet and share a stream.
A guest joins from their phone for ten minutes. A mod on another continent
watches everything and hits "BRB" when something goes wrong.

All of those devices need to agree on a few things: which show this is, who
is in it, who decides right now, what is on air, what is hidden, and what
each person lets the others see. stream-session is that agreement. It's small
on purpose: a handful of JSON messages, two counters and a few rules.

It covers:

- **one show id** that every device in a show shares (section 3);
- **the session record**: the show's shared state, held by one coordinator
  and sent in full to every member (section 5);
- **leases and epochs**: who coordinates, how that moves from device to
  device, and how old orders are fenced off (sections 7–8);
- **orders**, and the rule that **hiding always wins** (section 9);
- **owners, invites and grants**: several people's gear in one show, joining
  and leaving, revoking, guest feeds and shared ingests (section 10);
- **sharing levels**: what each owner lets each other owner see (section 11);
- an **optional coordination server** that anyone can host (section 13).

It doesn't cover:

- **what is wrong with a stream.** That's
  [stream-status](https://github.com/openstreambox/stream-status). Each
  device's status stays a stream-status snapshot, tagged with the show id
  (section 16). This spec defines no findings and no codes of its own.
- the media itself, ingest protocols, scenes and mixing;
- transport security and device pairing. Messages travel on channels that
  already exist between devices (a paired WebSocket, a remote-control
  relay). Those channels authenticate devices and encrypt traffic. This
  spec only adds end-to-end sealing for messages that a third party relays
  (section 14).

**No service is required.** A show with no server at all works: a planned
handover is an explicit two-step transfer, and a takeover after a device goes
silent happens only when the streamer taps yes. A coordination server, if
someone runs one, is just another member. It can hold a lease and forward
sealed messages, and it uses the same messages as everyone else.

## 2. Words

| Word | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **show** | One production: one output to the platform, from Go live to Ended. The id that names it is the **session**. |
| **owner** | A person (or a team acting as one) who controls some devices, feeds and ingests. Every show has exactly one **host** owner. Others are **co-streamers**, **guests** or **helpers**. |
| **device** | An encoder box, phone, PC, companion app, hub or server. A device's stream-status `source.id` is its identity across shows. |
| **member** | A device in one show. It gets a new **member id** for each show. |
| **feed** | One contribution to a show: "Kim's camera", "guest 1". A feed belongs to one owner. |
| **carrier** | The device sending a feed right now. It can change while the feed stays the same. |
| **mixer** | Where the show's feeds are combined and sent to the platform: a hub, a cloud OBS, the host's OBS, or, in a one-device show, the carrier itself. |
| **scope** | A thing that has one coordinator: the **show** scope, and one **owner** scope per owner (`owner:<owner id>`). |
| **coordinator** | The member that holds the lease of a scope and orders its decisions. The show coordinator is the mixer. Each owner's coordinator is one of that owner's devices. |
| **lease** | A coordinator's claim to a scope, valid for a few seconds and renewed by every send. |
| **epoch** | A number per scope that goes up by one every time the coordinator changes. |
| **grant** | What one owner allows another owner: actions on the issuer's things, and how much of the issuer's status the holder sees. |
| **invite** | A capability token an owner hands out (a QR code, a link) so someone can ask for a grant. |
| **projection** | What an owner sends another owner about the owner's own feeds, cut down to the level the owner chose. |
| **coordination server** | An optional member that holds leases as a witness and relays sealed messages (section 13). |

## 3. Identifiers

All ids are random. They MUST NOT be derived from a device, an account, a
platform handle, a network address, a serial number, an IMEI, a MAC address,
a key or a time.

| Id | Form | Made by | Lives |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `session` | 16 random bytes, 32 lowercase hex | Whoever goes live first, or the hub | Go live to Ended. It has the shape of a W3C trace-id, so exports can use it as an OpenTelemetry `session.id` without translation |
| `lookup` | 32 lowercase hex, derived from the session key (14.1) | Every member that has the key | The show. A coordination server sees only this, never `session` |
| `owner` | 8 random bytes, 16 hex | The owner's first device in this show | One show. A new show gets a new owner id unless the owner chooses to reuse one, so a host can't follow a co-streamer from show to show |
| `member` | 8 random bytes, 16 hex | The device, when it joins | One show. Kept when the device leaves and rejoins the same show |
| `feed` | 8 random bytes, 16 hex | The feed's owner | One show. One device in two shows has two feeds |
| `grant`, `invite` | 8 random bytes, 16 hex | The issuer | Until revoked or expired |
| `ingest` | 4–64 characters `[A-Za-z0-9_-]` | The ingest's owner | As long as the endpoint exists. It names the endpoint, never its address or key |

A device keeps its stream-status `source.id` across shows. That id appears
only in its own owner's `owner_state` (section 6). Everywhere else, and in
everything sent to other owners, the device is its member id.

`previous` MAY link a session to the one before it when a show really
restarted (a hub's daily cut, a crash with no way to continue), so a timeline
can join them. A device moving does **not** start a new session; that is
what epochs are for.

## 4. Messages

Every message is a JSON object (RFC 8259, UTF-8) with:

| Field | Required | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `format` | yes | `"stream-session"` |
| `v` | yes | `0` |
| `type` | yes | One of the types below |
| `generated` | no | RFC 3339 time on the sender's clock, for display and logs only |

| Type | From → to | Section |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `record` | Show coordinator → every member | 5 |
| `owner_state` | An owner's coordinator → that owner's devices | 6 |
| `transfer`, `ready`, `handed`, `moved` | Old and new coordinator of a scope | 8.1 |
| `claim`, `claim_result` | A candidate ↔ a coordination server | 8.3 |
| `hello`, `unknown_member` | A member ↔ the coordinator it found | 8.5 |
| `order` | Any member → a coordinator | 9 |
| `invite`, `join`, `grant`, `revoke`, `leave` | Between owners' coordinators | 10 |
| `projection` | One owner's coordinator → another owner's devices | 11 |
| `sealed` | Anyone → anyone, through a relay | 14 |

[`schema/stream-session.v0.schema.json`](../schema/stream-session.v0.schema.json)
defines every type. Fields starting with `x_<owner>_` are extensions (17).

**Clocks.** Freshness is always a duration measured on the reader's own
monotonic clock, from the moment it received a message. Readers MUST NOT
compare a sender's `generated` time, or any other wall-clock time, with
their own to decide whether something is current. A device that wakes from
sleep MUST treat every lease it held or followed as expired.

## 5. The session record

The show coordinator holds the record. It sends the whole record to every
member whenever something in it changes, and at least every 5 seconds. Each
send renews its lease. A member replaces its copy with each record; nothing
is merged from deltas, so a missed record costs nothing.

```json
{
  "format": "stream-session", "v": 0, "type": "record",
  "session": "4bf92f3577b34da6a3ce929d0e0e4736",
  "lease": { "scope": "show", "epoch": 3, "holder": "a3a3b4b4c5c5d6d6",
             "claimed": "transfer", "valid_for_ms": 15000, "lock_delay_ms": 10000 },
  "version": 41, "seq": 220, "key_gen": 0,
  "output": { "state": "live", "showing": "program", "since": "2026-11-15T14:00:00Z" },
  "owners": [ { "owner": "1a2b3c4d5e6f7081", "role": "host", "state": "joined", "label": "Kim" } ],
  "members": [
    { "member": "a3a3b4b4c5c5d6d6", "owner": "1a2b3c4d5e6f7081", "kind": "box",
      "caps": ["show.view", "show.hide", "show.mix"], "state": "joined" }
  ],
  "feeds": [
    { "feed": "f1f1e2e2d3d3c4c4", "owner": "1a2b3c4d5e6f7081", "carrier": "a3a3b4b4c5c5d6d6",
      "state": "open", "arriving": "up", "on_air": true }
  ]
}
```

| Field | Required | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `session` | yes | The show id |
| `previous` | no | The session this one continues after a real restart, or `null` |
| `lease` | yes | The show lease (7.1). `scope` is `show` |
| `version` | yes | Counts decisions within the epoch. **Order is the pair (epoch, version)** |
| `seq` | yes | Counts sends by this coordinator; a reader ignores a record whose `seq` is not newer than the last one from the same holder and epoch |
| `key_gen` | yes | Generation of the session key (14.1). Goes up when someone leaves or is removed |
| `output` | yes | The platform output (5.1) |
| `owners` | yes | Every owner in the show, with `role` (`host`, `co_streamer`, `guest`, `helper`), `state` (`joined`, `left`, `removed`) and an optional `label` |
| `members` | yes | Every member (5.2) |
| `feeds` | yes | Every feed (5.3) |
| `ingests` | no | Ingests the show uses (10.8) |
| `hides` | no | Hides in force (9.2) |
| `reach` | no | How to find the coordinator again (8.5) |

A record MUST name exactly one host. Left and removed owners and members
SHOULD stay in the record for 5 minutes, so every member sees that they
went, and MUST be dropped after 10.

### 5.1 `output`

`state` is `live` (the platform output is being delivered), `not_live` or
`unknown`. `showing` is `program` (the mixed show), `cover` (BRB or another
cover the show chose) or `unknown`. A show that shows a cover is still
**live**. Apps SHOULD say "live, showing BRB", never "not live" or "offline",
in that case. This is the same rule as stream-status's `live`.

### 5.2 Members

| Field | Required | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `member` | yes | Member id |
| `owner` | yes | Owner id, or `null` for a coordination server nobody in the show runs |
| `kind` | yes | `box`, `phone`, `pc`, `companion`, `hub`, `server`, `other` |
| `caps` | yes | Show rights: `show.view`, `show.hide`, `show.mix` (10.4) |
| `state` | yes | `joined`, `left`, `removed` |
| `grant` | no | For another owner's member: the host's grant that lets it in |
| `serves` | no | For a server: `witness`, `relay` |
| `heard_ms_ago` | no | How long ago the coordinator last heard from it, on its own clock |

The host's own devices carry the rights the host gave them when pairing.
Another owner's member MUST name a live grant from the host that includes
every right it lists. A server with `owner: null` MUST have no rights.

### 5.3 Feeds

| Field | Required | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `feed` | yes | Feed id |
| `owner` | yes | The feed's owner |
| `carrier` | yes | The member sending it now, always one of the owner's devices, or `null` |
| `state` | yes | `open`, `closed` |
| `arriving` | yes | What the mixer sees at its ingest: `up`, `down`, `unknown` |
| `on_air` | yes | Whether the feed is in the program right now |
| `ingest` | no | The ingest it arrives on |
| `label` | no | A name the feed's owner chose |

These are facts the mixer sees itself. What the feed's owner knows about
their own feed (privacy, findings, location) reaches others only through
projections, at the level the owner chose (section 11).

**Labels.** Owner and feed labels are only what the owner typed or
confirmed. Software MUST NOT fill a label from a device name, a Wi-Fi name, an
account name or a phone's contact card.

## 6. Owner state

Each owner's coordinator sends `owner_state` to that owner's own devices,
and to nobody else. It is the owner-side twin of the record:

| Field | Required | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `session`, `owner` | yes | The show and the owner |
| `lease` | yes | The owner lease. `scope` is `owner:<owner>` |
| `version`, `seq` | yes | As in the record |
| `feeds` | yes | The owner's feeds: `feed`, `carrier`, `state`, `privacy` (`on`, `off`) |
| `devices` | yes | The owner's devices: `member`, `source` (the stream-status `source.id`) and `kind` |
| `auto_failover` | yes | Whether the owner turned on automatic takeover through a coordination server (8.3) |
| `grants` | no | Ids of the grants this owner has issued |

The `source` of a device MUST NOT change when the device joins, leaves or
rejoins a show. Joining a show opens a member and feeds; it never changes who
the device is.

## 7. Leases, epochs and fencing

### 7.1 The lease

```json
{ "scope": "show", "epoch": 3, "holder": "a3a3b4b4c5c5d6d6",
  "claimed": "transfer", "valid_for_ms": 15000, "lock_delay_ms": 10000 }
```

- A lease is valid for `valid_for_ms` (5,000–60,000; default **15,000**)
  after a reader received the message carrying it, on the reader's clock.
  Each record or owner state from the holder renews it. The default sits
  above the 12-second timeout of NMOS IS-07, so a short cellular stall
  doesn't cause a takeover.
- When a lease runs out, a reader shows **"coordinator unknown"**. It keeps
  everything else as last known, marked stale.
- Nobody may claim the next epoch until a further `lock_delay_ms` (5,000–
  60,000; default **10,000**) has passed. With the defaults that's 25
  seconds of silence before a takeover can even be offered.
- `claimed` says how the epoch began: `start` (epoch 1 of a scope), `transfer`
  (a planned handover), `manual` (the streamer's tap, no witness) or
  `witness` (granted by a coordination server). A `manual` epoch tells
  everyone that the old coordinator may still be out there.

### 7.2 Two scopes

| Scope | Coordinator | Decides | Can't |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **show** | The mixer: a member with `show.mix`. In a one-device show with no hub, that's the carrier | What is on air, BRB and covers, Go live and End, who is in the show, the show's ingests | Show a feed its owner made private; change another owner's devices; see another owner's secrets or more than they share |
| **owner:&lt;id&gt;** | One of that owner's devices: normally their carrier, or their own hub | The owner's feeds: privacy, moving a feed between the owner's devices, ending a feed; the grants the owner issues; what the owner shares | Put anything on the show's air |

Each scope has its own epoch, and they move independently. When one device
does everything (a solo stream from a box), it holds both leases. A companion
app, or a phone that is only a remote, MUST NOT hold a lease: only a device
that carries media or mixes it can.

### 7.3 Order and fencing

- Every coordinator decision carries `(epoch, version)`. `version` counts
  decisions within an epoch and only goes up. Pairs compare epoch first.
- A member that sees a higher epoch for a scope than its own adopts it.
- A member that holds a lease and sees a **higher** epoch for the same scope
  **steps down at once**: it stops sending records for that scope and goes to
  standby. A carrier that steps down keeps its media running until the new
  coordinator decides; it never ends the broadcast on its own.
- Records and orders from a **lower** epoch are refused, except for hides
  (9.2).
- The parties that act on decisions check the epoch themselves: the carrier
  (it renders privacy and sends the media) and the mixer (it decides what is
  on air). Nobody else has to be trusted to fence.

## 8. Moving the coordinator

### 8.1 Planned handover (always safe, no server)

1. The coordinator of a scope at epoch *n* sends
   `transfer {scope, epoch: n+1, from, to}` to the new device. A default
   `timeout_ms` of 10,000 applies.
2. The new device answers `ready {scope, epoch: n+1, member}` once its own
   feed is healthy. With a mixer that can hold both feeds this is
   make-before-break; a single carrier without one has a short gap, and
   production projections say so (`handover.state: "gap"`).
3. The old coordinator sends `handed`, stops ordering at epoch *n*, and for
   30 seconds answers stragglers with `moved {scope, epoch: n+1, holder}`.
4. The new device sends its first record (or owner state) at epoch *n+1*
   with `claimed: "transfer"`.

If `ready` never comes, nothing has changed: the old coordinator keeps the
lease and the transfer is forgotten. There is no state in which nobody or
both coordinate.

### 8.2 Takeover without a server

When the coordinator falls silent, the other devices can't tell whether it
died or is only cut off: a box that lost the phone's Wi-Fi may still be
streaming on its own modems. Two devices can't decide that safely on their
own. So:

- No automatic takeover. After the lease and the lock-delay ran out (25
  seconds with the defaults), a device that can coordinate the scope MAY ask
  the streamer, in words like: "Kim's backpack hasn't answered for 25 s. It
  may still be streaming. Stream from this phone instead?"
- Only a yes from the streamer (or someone the owner trusts with that
  device) lets it claim epoch *n+1* with `claimed: "manual"`.
- A device MUST NOT claim before the lease and lock-delay ran out on its own
  clock, and MUST NOT skip an epoch.

### 8.3 Takeover with a coordination server

A coordination server (section 13) is a **witness**: it holds the lease
state of a scope and grants the next epoch to exactly one claimant.

1. While a coordinator holds a scope, each of its sends also reaches the
   server as a sealed envelope (14.2). The server treats it as a renewal.
2. When the lease and the lock-delay ran out **at the server**, a candidate
   sends `claim {lookup, scope, epoch: n+1, member, proof}`.
3. The server compares and sets: the first valid claim for *n+1* gets
   `claim_result {result: "granted"}`, every other one `refused`.
4. The winner sends its first record with `claimed: "witness"`.

A candidate MAY claim on its own only if the scope's owner turned on
`auto_failover`. Otherwise it still asks the streamer first and then
claims through the server, which still guarantees a single winner.

The server applies the `valid_for_ms` and `lock_delay_ms` that the owner
registered with it (defaults 15,000 and 10,000).

### 8.4 Split brain and conflicts

A partition can leave two coordinators: the old one, still at epoch *n* on
its side, and a new one at *n+1* on the other. That's expected and safe:

- Nothing the old side decides to **show** can be applied once the sides
  meet: it's fenced by the epoch.
- Everything the old side **hid** is kept (9.2).
- When the old coordinator sees *n+1*, it steps down and says so: "the phone
  took over at 14:31".

Two **manual** claims of the same epoch can happen when two devices were
both told to take over. Members that see two holders at one epoch MUST show
it (a stream-status finding, section 16) and MUST NOT resolve it silently.
Until it's resolved, members follow the holder with the higher member id, so
they at least follow the same one, and hides from both apply. The streamer
resolves it by picking one: the other hands over by a normal transfer at the
next epoch. Two holders of one epoch through `start`, `transfer` or
`witness` are a protocol error.

**Bench (two owners):** a partition while both devices stay online on
cellular, with and without a server; what two senders on one ingest look like
to viewers.

### 8.5 Finding the coordinator again

A member looks for the current coordinator in this order:

1. addresses it learnt while connected (fast on a LAN, useless after an
   address change);
2. LAN discovery, for example DNS-SD `_stream-session._tcp` (or an app's own
   service name). The TXT record carries only `v` and `path`: no session id,
   no owner, no device name;
3. the coordination server in the record's `reach` list (`via: "server"`,
   a `wss://` URL with a host name, never an IP address);
4. a `moved` redirect from the old coordinator.

On reconnecting, a member sends `hello {member, known: [{scope, epoch}]}`. A
coordinator that doesn't know the member answers `unknown_member`; the member
then joins again with its grant (10.6), as an NMOS node re-registers after a
404.

## 9. Orders, and why hiding always wins

### 9.1 Orders

A member asks a coordinator for a decision with
`order {scope, epoch, against, action, by}`, where `against` is the version it
saw. The coordinator applies it, raises `version`, and the result shows up
in its next record or owner state.

| Action | Scope | Kind | Who may send it |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `brb_on` | show | hide | `show.hide` or `show.mix` |
| `cancel_countdown` | show | hide | `show.hide` or `show.mix` |
| `off_air` (feed) | show | hide | `show.hide`, `show.mix`, or the feed's owner |
| `privacy_on` (feed) | owner | hide | The feed's owner, or a holder of the owner's `feed.hide` |
| `brb_off` | show | show more | `show.mix` |
| `on_air` (feed) | show | show more | `show.mix`, and only if the feed's owner granted the host `feed.on_air` |
| `go_live` | show | show more | `show.mix` |
| `privacy_off` (feed) | owner | show more | The feed's owner only |
| `move_feed` (feed, `to`) | owner | other | The feed's owner |
| `end_feed` (feed) | owner | other | The feed's owner |
| `end` | show | other | `show.mix` |

"Show more" and "other" orders apply only if `epoch` is the coordinator's
current epoch and `against` is its current version. Otherwise they're refused
as stale, so nobody acts on a picture that has changed since they saw it.

### 9.2 Hiding always wins

Showing less is always safe. Showing more needs the current coordinator.

- A **hide** (`brb_on`, `off_air`, `privacy_on`, `cancel_countdown`) from a
  permitted member applies **at once, in any epoch**: on a stale
  coordinator, on the current one, and on the carrier that renders it.
- A coordinator that receives a hide from a lower epoch (in a stale record,
  owner state or order) MUST keep it in its own records until someone
  permitted shows more again under the current epoch.
- A member that applied a hide from a lower epoch keeps it until a current
  record shows that the current coordinator released it.
- After a split, the union of all hides holds until the streamer shows more.
- **Privacy is enforced at the source.** The owner's carrier renders its own
  privacy cover, so a careless or hostile mixer can't show what the owner
  hid. The record's `privacy` hide and the projection's `picture: "covered"`
  let a mixer cut away, but safety never depends on the mixer doing so.

The record lists hides in force as `{kind, feed?, by, since}`, with `kind`
`brb` (the whole show), `off_air` or `privacy` (one feed). When `brb` is in
force, `output.showing` MUST NOT be `program`.

## 10. Owners, invites and grants

### 10.1 Who's who

The **host** owns the show: its platform output, its mixer and the ingests
it hands out. A **co-streamer** contributes feeds and usually shares more. A
**guest** contributes a feed for a while. A **helper** contributes nothing
but may watch or hide (a friend's mod, say).

Within one owner, devices trust each other through their own pairing (the
owner's existing app pairing, roles and tokens). Between owners, everything
goes through **grants**.

### 10.2 Invites

An owner who wants someone in creates an **invite**: a random capability
token of 32 bytes, written `sst0_` plus 43 base64url characters, plus a
public half:

```json
{ "format": "stream-session", "v": 0, "type": "invite",
  "session": "4bf9…", "invite": "d9d9c8c8b7b7a6a6", "issuer": "1a2b3c4d5e6f7081",
  "role": "guest",
  "offers": { "caps": ["show.view", "feed.contribute", "ingest.use"],
              "level": "minimal", "location": "off", "ingests": ["kim-guest"] },
  "uses": 1, "ttl_ms": 900000, "issued": "2026-11-15T14:00:00Z" }
```

- The token travels **only** in the QR code or link the issuer shows or
  sends, for example `stream-session:0?s=<session>&i=<invite>&t=<token>&r=<server url>`.
  It never appears in any message. Anyone holding it can use it, so it is
  short-lived and counted. `uses` is one per device the invited owner may
  bring (default 1, at most 16); after the first use, the invite admits only
  more devices of the same owner. `ttl_ms` defaults
  to 5 minutes for a QR code on screen and 15 minutes for a link, at most
  24 hours.
- The issuer SHOULD also show a six-digit code on both screens when the two
  devices are side by side, as the owner's own pairing does.
- Without a server, the invitee connects straight to the issuer's
  coordinator (the address in the QR code or found on the LAN). With a
  server, the link's `r` names it.

### 10.3 Joining

The invitee's device sends `join {invite, proof, owner, member, kind, label?}`.
`proof` is HMAC-SHA256 with the token as key over
`"stream-session v0 join|" + session + "|" + invite + "|" + member`, in hex.
It proves the device holds the token without sending it. The invitee makes
its own `owner` and `member` ids; the owner's further devices reuse that
owner id. The issuer checks the proof, the invite's expiry, its use count and
that every use after the first comes from the same owner. Another owner's
device is listed in the record only after it joined.

### 10.4 Grants

A grant is what one owner (the **issuer**) allows another owner (the
**holder**) for the rest of the show:

```json
{ "format": "stream-session", "v": 0, "type": "grant",
  "session": "4bf9…", "grant": "e4e4d5d5c6c6b7b7", "invite": null, "parent": null,
  "issuer": "2b3c4d5e6f708192", "holder": "1a2b3c4d5e6f7081",
  "caps": ["feed.on_air", "feed.hide"], "level": "production", "location": "off",
  "ttl_ms": 600000, "issued": "2026-11-15T14:00:00Z", "renewal": 0, "state": "active" }
```

- `caps`: actions the holder may take on the issuer's things:

  | Cap | Means | Usually from → to |
  | --- | --- | --- |
  | `show.view` | Receive the show's record | Host → everyone in the show |
  | `show.hide` | BRB, cancel a countdown, take any feed off air | Host → mods, co-streamers |
  | `show.mix` | Hold the show lease: decide what is on air, go live, end | Host → their mixer (a remote producer's PC, a hub) |
  | `feed.contribute` | Open feeds in the show | Host → co-streamers, guests |
  | `ingest.use` | Get a show-scoped credential for the listed `ingests` | Host → co-streamers, guests |
  | `feed.on_air` | Put the issuer's feeds on air | Feed owner → host |
  | `feed.hide` | Turn privacy on for the issuer's feeds (never off) | Feed owner → host, a trusted mod |
  | `grant.attenuate` | Pass a narrower copy of this grant on | Anyone → someone they trust to delegate |

- `level` and `location`: how much of the issuer's status the holder sees
  (section 11). `none` means no projection at all.
- **Both sides consent.** A guest is in the show when the host granted them
  `show.view` and `feed.contribute`; their feed goes on air only once they
  granted the host `feed.on_air`. Nobody can put another owner's feed on air
  without that.
- **Short-lived.** `ttl_ms` is at most 10 minutes. The issuer re-sends the
  grant with `renewal` + 1 at least every 5 minutes while the holder is in
  the show. A grant that isn't renewed expires on its own, on the holder's
  and issuer's clocks, even if a revoke never arrived. That is the real
  revocation; an explicit `revoke` makes it immediate.
- A grant made from an invite MUST NOT give more than the invite offered.

### 10.5 Passing a grant on

A holder of a grant with `grant.attenuate` MAY issue a **child** grant to a
third owner, with `parent` set to the original. The child's caps, level,
location, ingests and ttl MUST each be within the parent's. When the parent
is revoked or expires, every child goes with it. This lets a host hand
"may hide Ari's feed" to a mod without asking Ari each time, and Ari can
still take it back in one step.

### 10.6 Leaving, removing and revoking

- **Leaving:** a member sends `leave`. Its feeds close; its owner may keep
  other devices in the show. The member id stays reserved, so if the device
  comes back while its owner's grant is still live it rejoins as the same
  member. Its `source` never changes (section 6).
- **Removing:** the issuer sends `revoke {grant, by, reason: "removed"}`. A
  holder MAY revoke its own grant with `reason: "left"`.
- When an owner's last host grant with `show.view` ends, the owner is
  **removed**: its members and the owner are marked `removed`, its feeds
  close and go off air, its show-scoped ingest credentials are revoked at the
  ingest, and records stop going to its devices.
- After anyone leaves or is removed, the show coordinator MUST rotate the
  session key and raise `key_gen` before its next record (14.1). Later
  records are then unreadable to them.
- When a feed owner revokes the host's `feed.on_air`, the mixer MUST take that
  owner's feeds off air in its next record.
- **What someone already saw can't be recalled.** Apps SHOULD say so when
  someone shares more, not only when they revoke.

**Bench (two owners):** revoking a guest mid-show, with the guest's feed on
air through a shared ingest: time until the feed is off air and the ingest
credential refused.

### 10.7 Guest feeds

A guest gets `feed.contribute` and `ingest.use` for one ingest. The host's
ingest hands the guest a **show-scoped credential**: single-use, expiring
with the grant, revoked on removal. How that credential travels is the
ingest's own business (its API, sealed to the guest); this spec only names
the ingest and the grant it belongs to. A guest never receives the host's
platform key. A platform token handed to a helper inside one owner's team
(some remote-control apps do this) MUST NOT be relayed across owners.

A guest's view is what the host shares with them (often `minimal`) plus the
show record, and their own feed's health from their own devices.

### 10.8 Shared ingests

An ingest is a receiving endpoint (SRT, SRTLA, RTMP, WHIP, RIST) owned by
one owner, usually the host:

```json
{ "ingest": "kim-srtla", "owner": "1a2b3c4d5e6f7081", "protocol": "srtla",
  "shared": true, "feeds": ["f1f1e2e2d3d3c4c4", "f2f2e3e3d4d4c5c5"] }
```

- The record names it by its id, never by its address or key.
- `shared: true` lists the feeds from other owners allowed on it. A
  non-shared ingest carries only its owner's feeds.
- Each feed on a shared ingest uses its own credential, so one can be revoked
  without touching the others.

**Bench (two owners):** two owners' senders on one shared ingest (an SRT
server, MediaMTX, a cloud relay); and two senders that both believe they
carry the same feed. What the receiver does, what viewers see.

### 10.9 Who coordinates what

| Decision | Who | How |
| --- | --- | --- |
| What is on air, scenes, BRB, Go live, End | The show's mixer | Show lease |
| Who is in the show | The host | Host grants |
| A feed's privacy, its carrier, ending it | The feed's owner | Owner lease |
| What another owner may do with my feeds | Me | My grants |
| What another owner sees of me | Me | My grants' level and location |
| Hiding something right now | Anyone permitted, in any epoch | Hide orders |

## 11. Sharing levels and projections

**Privacy is the streamer's call.** Nothing is guessed private or public,
and nothing is restricted by region. Each owner chooses, per show and per
other owner, how much the others see.

### 11.1 Levels

| Level | The other owner sees, per feed |
| --- | --- |
| `none` | Nothing beyond the show-side facts in the record (on air, arriving) |
| `minimal` | Up, down or unknown; the camera or a cover ("private, or not the camera"); the highest active severity |
| `crew` | Minimal, plus the owner's findings for that feed (per link and per feed), as stream-status snapshots with an empty `checked` list |
| `production` | Crew, plus everything the show's mixer needs to run the show: the full snapshots including `checked`, the carrier's device kind, a coming handover and its expected gap, the ingest, and the media settings (codec, size, frame rate, bitrate) |

**Location** is a separate switch with three positions, `off`, `area` (a
place name the owner picked, like a city) and `live` (coordinates). It is
**off at every level** until the owner turns it on for a person in a show.

**Defaults.** When an owner joins a show, their app SHOULD preselect
`minimal` and location `off` for every other owner, show that choice, and
let the owner confirm or change it before anything is shared. Nothing
beyond the show-side facts is shared before that.

### 11.2 Projections

```json
{ "format": "stream-session", "v": 0, "type": "projection",
  "session": "4bf9…", "from": "2b3c4d5e6f708192", "to": "1a2b3c4d5e6f7081",
  "grant": "e4e4d5d5c6c6b7b7", "level": "minimal", "shares_location": "off",
  "seq": 1, "valid_for_ms": 12000,
  "feeds": [ { "feed": "f2f2e3e3d4d4c5c5", "state": "up", "picture": "camera", "worst": "info" } ] }
```

- **The owner's side cuts it down.** The owner's coordinator builds each
  projection for one recipient owner, at that recipient's grant level. The
  recipient never receives the rest, so it can't leak it.
- A projection's `level` MUST NOT be above its grant's, and
  `shares_location` MUST NOT be above the grant's `location`. A `location`
  field appears only when `shares_location` allows it: `area` carries a
  place name and no coordinates; `live` carries `lat`, `lon`, optional
  `accuracy_m` and `as_of`.
- **Missing means unknown, never fine.** A recipient treats anything a
  projection doesn't carry as unknown, and a projection older than its
  `valid_for_ms` as stale. Senders send on change and at least every 5
  seconds.
- **Embedded snapshots** (crew and production) are stream-status v0
  snapshots. For use across owners they MUST:
  - set `source.id` to the device's **member id**, not its install id;
  - set `session_id` to this show's session;
  - leave out subject `label`s, which can be private (a Wi-Fi network name);
  - at the crew level, carry `"checked": []`.

  Validate them with the stream-status tools. This spec adds no fields to
  them.
- A projection relayed through anyone other than the recipient (the mixer,
  a server) MUST be sealed with that grant's key (14.3), so the relay can't
  read it.
- A coordinator MUST NOT send a projection under a grant that is revoked or
  expired, and MUST NOT deliver it to anyone outside the holder owner.

**Bench (two owners):** a mixer and two owners at different levels for each
other; check that each one receives exactly its level, and that location
appears only where turned on.

## 12. What never appears

No message, record, projection or extension field may contain:

- stream keys, platform keys, ingest credentials, SRT passphrases or stream
  ids, RTMP or SRT URLs with credentials;
- OAuth or platform tokens, invite tokens, member tokens, session keys,
  grant keys;
- Wi-Fi passwords, SIM PINs or PUKs;
- IP addresses, device serial numbers, IMEIs, MAC addresses, phone numbers,
  email addresses;
- location, except in a projection's `location` field as allowed above.

The validator rejects fields named like secrets (`stream_key`, `password`,
`token`, `pin` and so on) anywhere, values that look like keys, tokens, URLs
with credentials, IP or email addresses, and location fields outside a
projection. Nothing about the session goes into the video (SEI) in v0: a
session id in the picture would follow every recording and re-upload.

## 13. The optional coordination server

A coordination server is a small, self-hostable process. Anyone can run one:
the host, a co-streamer, a community. It's a member like any other and adds
no message of its own.

| Role | What it does | What it sees |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **witness** | Holds lease state per scope, grants the next epoch to exactly one claimant (8.3) | Lookup keys, scopes, epochs, timings, member ids, ciphertext sizes |
| **relay** | Forwards sealed messages between members that can't reach each other (cellular NAT, client-isolated Wi-Fi) | The same |
| **holder** | Holds a lease itself, like a hub | Only when an owner runs it as one of their own devices: then it is that owner's member, with that owner's keys |

- A server that nobody in the show runs (`owner: null`) never holds show
  rights and never sees the session id, the record or any projection in the
  clear.
- It authenticates claims with a **claim key** per scope (14.1) that only
  devices allowed to coordinate that scope have. The first holder registers
  the scope with the server and gives it the claim key and the lease timings.
- **What it can and can't do.** A server can refuse service or, if
  malicious, grant a lease to the wrong one of the owner's devices. It can't
  read records, can't put anything on air, and can't undo a hide: hides apply
  from any epoch, and privacy is rendered on the owner's own carrier.
- It stores nothing but the current lease per scope and the sealed messages
  in flight. Keeping it small enough to rewrite in a day is a goal.

Collabs between people in different places will usually want one, because
their devices rarely share a LAN. It is still optional: a show without it
loses only automatic failover and relaying across NAT.

**Bench (two owners):** witness failover with automatic failover on, and a
relay between two owners on different cellular networks.

## 14. Keys and sealing

### 14.1 The session key

- The show coordinator makes 32 random bytes with the session and gives them
  to members only over their paired channels. Each rotation makes a new key
  and raises `key_gen`.
- `lookup` = the first 16 bytes, as hex, of
  HKDF-SHA256(key generation 0, salt empty, info `"stream-session v0 lookup"`).
  It stays the same for the whole show, so a server can keep the lease
  across rotations.
- Seal key for generation *g* = HKDF-SHA256(key *g*, info
  `"stream-session v0 seal"`).
- Claim key for a scope = HKDF-SHA256(a scope secret, info
  `"stream-session v0 claim|" + scope`). The show's scope secret goes only
  to members with `show.mix`; each owner's scope secret only to that owner's
  devices. Claim `proof` is HMAC-SHA256(claim key,
  `"stream-session v0 claim|" + lookup + "|" + scope + "|" + epoch + "|" + member`).
- A removed member still knows `lookup`, so it could watch timings at a
  server. It can't read anything or claim anything.

### 14.2 Sealed envelopes

```json
{ "format": "stream-session", "v": 0, "type": "sealed",
  "lookup": "9c1d55e0a7b24f0e8f3a6b2c1d0e9f8a", "scope": "show", "epoch": 4, "key_gen": 2,
  "to": "all", "alg": "chacha20-poly1305",
  "nonce": "q1w2e3r4t5y6u7i8", "ct": "…" }
```

- ChaCha20-Poly1305 (RFC 8439), a random 12-byte nonce (base64url), the
  plaintext a complete stream-session message, and the envelope's other
  fields, serialised as JSON, as associated data.
- `to` is `all` (sealed with the session seal key) or a grant id (sealed with
  that grant's key, for one owner).
- After a removal, nothing may be sealed with an older `key_gen`.

### 14.3 Grant keys

Each grant has its own 32-byte key, made by the issuer and sent with the
grant over the channel the grant travels on (direct, or sealed for the
holder). Projections relayed through a mixer or a server are sealed with it.
It ends with the grant.

## 15. Rules for every member

These keep any member from showing a better picture than the truth.

1. **Freshness on your own clock** (section 4). A record or owner state is
   current for its lease's `valid_for_ms`; a projection for its
   `valid_for_ms`. After that, show it as unknown, never as fine.
2. **Replace, don't merge.** Each record replaces the last one from the same
   scope. Each projection replaces the last one from the same owner.
3. **Order.** Ignore lower epochs (except their hides), and within an epoch
   ignore anything not newer by `version` or `seq`.
4. **Unknown fields** are ignored (minor versions add fields).
5. **Unknown values never look better:**
   - unknown `output.state` or `showing` → `unknown`;
   - unknown `claimed` → treat as `manual`;
   - unknown feed `state`, `arriving` or projection `state`/`picture` →
     `unknown`; unknown `worst` → `warning`;
   - unknown projection `level` → read only the minimal fields;
   - unknown hide `kind` → treat the feed (or the show) as hidden;
   - unknown order `action` → refuse it;
   - unknown cap → ignore it (it grants nothing).
6. **Invalid messages** are refused as a whole; the last good state goes stale
   on its own. A member SHOULD report it as a finding (section 16).

## 16. Findings: how this uses stream-status

stream-status is the format for "what is wrong, where, and is it still
live". stream-session doesn't repeat any of it. It uses it like this:

- Every device keeps publishing its own stream-status snapshots. In a show it
  sets the snapshot's `session_id` to the session. Readers use it to group
  snapshots from one show; the coordinator relays the latest snapshot per
  device, **unmerged**.
- Across owners, snapshots travel only inside projections, under the rules in
  11.2.
- Conditions this spec makes visible are reported as stream-status findings
  by whichever device sees them. Until codes for them are registered in the
  stream-status registry, devices use extension codes there, for example
  `x_streamsession.session.coordinator_unknown` with subject kind
  `x_streamsession_coordinator`. The conditions are:
  - no current lease for a scope;
  - two holders of one scope at the same epoch, or two coordinators within
    their leases;
  - two devices both sending for one feed or one platform output;
  - a transfer that got no `ready` within its timeout;
  - a member not heard from within its freshness window;
  - a grant that expired while its feed was on air.

Severities, recovery and wording for those findings follow stream-status. A
proposal to register them will go to the stream-status repository; nothing
in this spec depends on it.

## 17. Versioning and extensions

- `v` is the major version. v0 is a draft: fields can still change between v0
  drafts, each with a changelog entry and updated fixtures.
- From v1 on, changes within a major version are additive only: new optional
  fields, new types, new caps, new actions and new enum values. The rules in
  section 15 keep older readers safe.
- Extension fields start with `x_<owner>_`. They are subject to section 12
  like everything else.

## 18. Conformance

- A **sender** conforms if every message it sends passes
  `tools/stream_session.py validate`: the JSON Schema plus the checks it can't
  express (one host, other owners' members naming a grant, carriers belonging
  to the feed's owner, hides consistent with the output, projection levels and location,
  embedded snapshot rules, and the privacy scan of section 12).
- A **set of devices** conforms if traces of what they send pass
  `tools/stream_session.py sequence`, which plays a reference model of every
  member: lease justification (start, transfer, the streamer's tap after the
  lease and lock-delay, a witness grant), stepping down, hides kept across
  epochs, stale orders refused, grants backed by invites and parents,
  revocation and key rotation, and projections within their grants.
- The fixtures in [`fixtures/`](../fixtures/README.md) are the test cases.

## 20. Prior art

Informative. v0 borrows on purpose:

- **stream-status** (<https://github.com/openstreambox/stream-status>): the
  findings format this spec relies on, and its rules: unknown is never
  healthy, the reader judges freshness, replace don't merge, additive
  versions, `x_<owner>` extensions.
- **NOALBS** (<https://github.com/NOALBS/nginx-obs-automatic-low-bitrate-switching>,
  docs at <https://docs.noalbs.com>): the open-source scene switcher many IRL
  setups run, and the closest thing the scene has to a shared contract
  between receivers and OBS. stream-session sits next to it: NOALBS switches
  scenes from receiver stats; stream-session says which show those feeds
  belong to and who decides. A NOALBS-style switcher could be one of the
  mixer's members.
- **Raft** (Ongaro and Ousterhout): terms as a logical clock, adopting the
  higher term, stepping down, and the reason two parties can't elect alone.
- **Chubby** (Burrows, OSDI 2006): sequencers checked by the resource, and the
  lock-delay. **etcd** and **NATS JetStream**: leases and compare-and-set.
  Martin Kleppmann's fencing tokens.
- **AMWA NMOS IS-04**: registries, heartbeats, and re-registering after a
  404. **IS-07**: the 5-second heartbeat and 12-second timeout.
- **DNS-SD (RFC 6763)**: small TXT records. **Home Assistant config flows**:
  discovered is not trusted.
- **OAuth 2.0 scopes (RFC 6749)**, **Biscuit** and **macaroons**: named,
  narrowable rights. **LiveKit** tokens: per-participant flags, opaque ids,
  and short-lived grants as the real revocation. **Matrix** power levels:
  rights kept in the shared state.
- **IRLToolkit's ingest sharing** for cloud OBS: both sides consent, time
  boxed by default.
- **W3C Trace Context** and **OpenTelemetry**'s `session.id`: the id shape,
  and no personal data in ids.
- **WHIP (RFC 9725)** session URLs and libsrt's single-use stream id session
  key: show-scoped ingest credentials.
- **RFC 8439** (ChaCha20-Poly1305) and **RFC 5869** (HKDF).
